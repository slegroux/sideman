# 6. Sound design: racks, macros, sends, and the plug-in wall

**You'll have:** an effect rack on the Lead with a filter and a ping-pong
delay on two macros, the pad feeding the reverb, and one third-party plug-in
loaded on the master — with an honest account of what Claude can and cannot
touch on it.

## Say

> Put an effect rack on the Lead with a filter and a ping-pong delay, and
> expose cutoff and delay feedback as macros. Send the pad to the reverb
> return. Load Pro-Q on the master and tell me what you can control.

## What Claude does

Loads the devices from the browser, adds macros and maps them, sets the send,
selects the master track and loads the plug-in there, then reads the plug-in's
parameter list — and tells you the truth about it.

## What you'll see in Live

The rack on the Lead with two named macros. On the master, Pro-Q — with only
**Device On** showing in Live's parameter list until you press **Configure**
on the device and click the controls you want exposed.

## Check

Ask: *"what can you change on Pro-Q right now?"* — one parameter. Press
Configure, click three controls in the plug-in window, then ask again: three
more. That button is the one thing Claude cannot press for you.

::: {.callout-note}
## The plug-in wall
Live only shows a plug-in's controls to scripts once you've configured them.
Claude can read the plug-in's full parameter names and tell you exactly what
to expose; it cannot press the button. Live's own devices have no such wall.
:::
