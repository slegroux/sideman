# 2. Drums: the kick is the clock

**You'll have:** a Drums track with a 909 kit and a four-bar groove — kick on
every beat, clap on 2 and 4, open hats off the beat, closed hats on 16ths
with a little movement — looping.

## Say

> Make a Drums track with a 909 kit. Kick on every beat, claps on 2 and 4,
> open hats off-beat, closed hats on 16ths with a little velocity movement.
> Four bars, then loop it.

## What Claude does

Creates the track, finds a 909 kit in Live's browser and loads it, creates a
four-bar clip in the first slot, writes the whole pattern in one go, then
reads it back and shapes the closed-hat velocities so they alternate.

## What you'll see in Live

A new track at the end named Drums with the kit in its device chain, and a
clip in scene 1. Open the clip: the kick row on every quarter, claps on 2
and 4, hats on every 16th with alternating velocity bars.

## Check

Ask: *"how many notes are in the drum clip?"* — 40 for this pattern. Play it
and listen for the off-beat open hat.

::: {.callout-note}
## Notes are the one thing Claude edits with special tools
Everything else in Live is a thing Claude reads and writes by name. MIDI
notes are the exception: they go in and come out through note tools built
for them, which is why Claude can edit velocities in place afterwards.
:::
